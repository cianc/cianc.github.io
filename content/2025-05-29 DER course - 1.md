## Notes
+ DERs: controllable electrical devices that plug in at the edge of the grid
+ There is a range of step-down voltages for customers on the distribution network. From O(10kV) to 120/240V for domestic consumers.
+ Solar array angle can be such that total daily output is approximately constant between seasons. Tilted more towards sun during winter and consequently less during summer.
+ Cost for solar has gone down dramatically across the board, BUT utility scale has gone down much more, largely because of bigger decreases in soft costs: tax, profit, labour, and also scaling costs for hardware
+ Cost ration of ~3:1 for cell:pack in battery cost
+ EV's are "batteries on wheels" from a modelling perspective.
+ Up to ~60% increase in demand peak due to EV charging

+ https://public.emdat.be/data has global disaster data sets, but just the data, no graphics.
+ https://ourworldindata.org/natural-disasters#explore-data-on-natural-disasters uses EM-DAT. https://ourworldindata.org/disaster-database-limitations points out the limitations of EM-DAT including undersampling of smaller disasters in the past, lots of missing data for economic impacts and separately heat deaths, and the difficulty in estimating indirect impacts.
+ https://ourworldindata.org/us-weather-climate - min temperatures have increased faster than max (so night has increased more than day and winter more than summer), more floods and more droughts
+ GHG emissions in US well off track for Paris goals by 2050. Agriculture and buildings and flat/up, industry and transport down modestly, but electricity generation is the hero [![[us_total_co2_emissions.png]]](https://kevinjkircher.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/der-introduction.pdf)
